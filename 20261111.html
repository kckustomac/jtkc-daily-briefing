Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 11, 2026 · fitting for Veterans Day

valorous

adjective · \VAL-uh-russ\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Someone or something described as valorous is courageous or brave.

// For carrying three wounded members of his squadron out of harm’s way, the lieutenant was presented with an award that recognized his valorous actions in the heat of battle.

In context: “… a Texas native who was awarded the Medal of Honor for valorous action during combat in Vietnam.” — Melissa Renteria, The San Antonio (Texas) Express-News, 8 Mar. 2026

Did you know? English has two words for brave from the same Latin verb, valēre (“to be well, to have strength”): valiant, borrowed from Anglo-French in the 1300s, and valorous, which followed in the 1400s from valor plus the suffix -ous. Valorous often has an archaic or romantic ring, describing stout-hearted warriors of yore.

Thune says the lame duck will focus on nominees and funding the government; the bipartisan permitting bill that cleared a 73-25 Senate vote Monday is still pending.

Courts: No major ongoing U.S. criminal trial was verified from today’s sources. At the Supreme Court, the November sitting’s argument days are done for this week (no arguments are scheduled after Tuesday, and today is a federal holiday). Sun Valley Orchards, on whether the government can fine a New Jersey farm over H-2A farmworker conditions through its own proceedings instead of a jury trial. Rulings are expected by summer.
