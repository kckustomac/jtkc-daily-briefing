On This Day

November 12, 1799

First meteor shower recorded in North America. Astronomer Andrew Ellicott, aboard a ship off the Florida Keys, watched the Leonids light up the sky and wrote that “the whole heaven appeared as if illuminated with sky rockets.” His journal entry is the earliest known record of a meteor shower in North America, and the Leonids’ 1833 storm later helped launch the study of meteor astronomy.

Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 12, 2026

gauntlet

noun · \GAWNT-lut\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: A gauntlet is a line, series, or assemblage of people or things that poses some sort of ordeal. It originally meant a double file of men armed with clubs, facing each other, with a person forced to run between them.

// Salmon returning to their spawning grounds must make it past a gauntlet of predators, from eagles to otters to bears.

In context: “… I’m also a mother in my forties, which means daily life is a gauntlet of logistics: lunches, permission slips, orthodontist appointments, emotional squalls, school emails multiplying like fruit flies.” — Mira Ptacin, Literary Hub, 4 Feb. 2026

Did you know? The original English word for the punishment was gantelope, from the Swedish gatlopp, literally “run down the lane.” The odd-sounding gantelope morphed into the familiar gauntlet through folk etymology, even though a separate, older gauntlet (a glove worn with medieval armor, from Middle French gantelet) had already been in English for about 200 years.
