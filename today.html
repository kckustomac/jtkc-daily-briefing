Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 28, 2026

parse · verb · \PARSS\

Meaning: To parse or parse through something is to study it by looking closely at its parts, or in other words to analyze it critically. In grammar and linguistics, parse means “to divide (a sentence) into grammatical parts and identify the parts and their relations to each other.”

Example: The lawyer meticulously parsed the wording of the final contract to be sure that her client would get all that he was asking for.

In context: “The stakes will be high for the summit, and every word out of each leader’s mouth will be parsed.” — Kevin Breuninger, CNBC, 12 May 2026

Did you know? Parse comes from the first element of the Latin term for “part of speech,” pars orationis, and has been used since at least the mid-1500s.
