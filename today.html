Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 5, 2026

effrontery

noun · \ih-FRUN-tuh-ree\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Effrontery is a formal word that refers to bold shamelessness, or in other words an attitude or way of behaving that is both confident and shocking or rude.

// Holly could not believe the sheer effrontery of the student who asked that her grade be changed even though she had completed little of the coursework.

In context: “Big Nose kicks up a loud fuss when a squirrel, chipmunk or bird has the effrontery to cross on the deck while he is lazily laying with his eyes peeking out the patio door glass.” — Barry Ellsworth, The Trenton (Ontario) Trentonian, 4 June 2026

Did you know? Effrontery is a formal cousin of nerve, cheek and gall. It traces back to Medieval Latin effrons, from ex- (“without”) plus frons (“forehead”), which meant “shameless.” One theory: frons could stand for the ability to blush, so someone with effrontery shows no visible embarrassment.

Kansas orders close-race audits as governor's race stays too close to call

Secretary of State Scott Schwab ordered close-race audits in six races decided by 1% or less on election night, including governor, secretary of state and attorney general, reviewing ballots from 10% of precincts in each county. Republicans keep control of the Kansas House but lose the 84-seat supermajority they used to override Gov. Laura Kelly's vetoes, so if Democrat Cindy Holscher wins the governor's race, her vetoes would be more likely to hold. As of Wednesday night, the AP had called 48 seats for Democrats and 64 for Republicans, with 13 still uncalled as late mail ballots arrive through Friday.
