Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 4, 2026

fulsome

adjective · \FULL-sum\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Fulsome describes someone or something that is generous in amount, extent, or spirit.

// They were fulsome in their praise of her efforts to raise money for the new wing of the children’s hospital.

In context: “... her archive, now kept at Yale University, forms the basis of this lusty, vibrant and fittingly multi-textured bio-doc that pays fulsome tribute to [Barbara] Hammer’s shapeshifting, once undervalued but now increasingly revered talent.” — Leslie Felperin, The Hollywood Reporter, 4 Sept. 2026

Did you know? In the 19th century, fulsome mostly meant excessive, insincere flattery, and that sense is still around. Since the early 1900s it has increasingly meant “abundant, copious” (“fulsome detail”), which is actually its original 14th-century meaning. So “fulsome praise” can mean generous praise or sucking up, and this morning's victory and concession speeches offered plenty of both.

Congress: Election night went heavily to Democrats: C-SPAN's front page is a run of Democratic victory speeches and Republican concessions, and House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries spoke as his party was on track to win the House in January. Both chambers stay out this week except for a Senate pro forma session Thursday. The Senate returns Monday, Nov. 9 at 3 p.m. with a cloture vote around 5:30 p.m. to start debate on H.R. 2347, the vehicle for the bipartisan permitting reform deal. The lame duck's must-do list: fund the government before the stopgap runs out on Dec. 11, finish the NDAA, and extend the farm and surface transportation laws, plus a push to confirm nominees before the new Congress arrives.

Courts: This morning the Supreme Court hears its only argument of the day, Guerrero v. Johnson: Texas death-row prisoner Dexter Johnson argues that updated medical standards for diagnosing intellectual disability finally gave him a workable claim under Atkins v. Virginia, so he should get a second federal habeas petition. Texas, backed by the U.S. Solicitor General, says Atkins was available all along. Yesterday the Justices heard St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy (religious preschools in Colorado's universal pre-K program), and the border-wall emergency application Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church still has no order, with the docket unchanged since the Oct. 30 reply.
