Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 19, 2026
Wordupbraid
Pronunciationup-BRAYD

verb — to speak to someone in an angry or critical way in response to something they did wrong; to scold them.

“The teacher upbraided the class after discovering the chalkboard erasers had been clapped all over the walls.”

In context: “An industrious ant spends all summer gathering food for the cold months, while a lazy grasshopper dances and sings. Once winter hits, the grasshopper is cold and hungry, and goes begging for food from the ant, who refuses to share any of its bounty, upbraiding the grasshopper for failing to plan.” — David A. Graham, The Atlantic, 17 Apr. 2026

Did you know? Upbraid is not related to topknot, but the braid in it likely shares a source with the hair-weaving verb. Old English bregdan meant “to snatch,” “to move suddenly,” or “to plait,” and ūpbregdan likely combined it with up — a formal cousin of getting in someone's face.
