On This Day

November 18 · 1883

U.S. railroads invent standard time zones. On this day in 1883, North American railroads adopted four standard time zones, ending the era when most cities ran on their own local sun time. Private railways drew the lines to make schedules workable, and those lines are very close to today's time zones. Your 4 AM alarm owes them a debt.

Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 18, 2026
Wordhobbyhorse
PronunciationHAH-bee-horss

noun — a topic that someone dwells on, returning to again and again, especially in conversation.

“Once he gets on his hobbyhorse and starts talking about taxes, you can't get him to discuss anything else.”

In context: “This has been a hobbyhorse of mine for a while. Boston should rechristen Logan Airport to honor the unparalleled [Bill] Russell.” — Mark Leibovich, The Atlantic, 14 June 2024

Did you know? Hobby is a shortening of hobbyhorse, which itself comes from an older hobby meaning a small or medium-sized horse (1400s). It went from a morris-dance horse costume to a toy stick-horse to a favorite pastime, and finally to a subject someone rides again and again.
