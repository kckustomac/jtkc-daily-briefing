Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 9, 2026

wabi-sabi

noun · \WAH-bee-SAH-bee\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Wabi-sabi refers to a philosophy of Japanese origin that emphasizes the aesthetic value in imperfect, impermanent, and simple things. The word can also refer to the qualities of imperfection, impermanence, and simplicity considered as beautiful.

// Over the years, the gardener learned to let go of his perfectionism and embrace wabi-sabi.

In context: “Traditional Japanese homes often follow the philosophy of wabi-sabi, an appreciation for imperfection and asymmetry. If elements are flawed, worn out or unbalanced, rather than scrambling to fix them, the theory goes, it’s better to not only accept but celebrate a place’s peccadillos.” — Robin Roberts, The Vancouver (British Columbia) Sun, 9 May 2026

Did you know? Wabi-sabi was borrowed directly from Japanese in the early 1960s, but the philosophy is much older. In the mid-16th century, tea master Sen Rikyū established simple, unadorned beauty (wabi) and an aged, rustic patina (sabi) as core aesthetics of tea-ceremony ceramics. A popular expression is kintsugi, the art of repairing broken pottery with lacquer and metal powder to highlight, rather than hide, the repair.

Wyandotte County and Kansas settle Chiefs stadium dispute

The Unified Government will pledge sales tax and part of its hotel-tax revenue from a STAR bond district limited to the new stadium and its immediate surroundings, and will permanently drop its October lawsuit against the state. In return, Mayor Christal Watson gets a voting seat on the Kansas Sports Facilities Authority, the board overseeing the $3 billion domed stadium, and Kansas will carve the Quindaro Ruins area out of its STAR bond district.

Democrats break Missouri’s 14-year GOP supermajority

Democrats flipped at least 16 Missouri House seats and two key Senate seats, so Republicans keep their majorities but lose the two-thirds margin that let them override vetoes and pass emergency-clause laws without Democratic votes. The Kansas House lost its GOP supermajority, too; Democrat Keri Ingle won the Lee’s Summit and Blue Springs Senate seat.

A PortKC committee approved the Country Club Plaza bond plan, the second of three approvals, with final bond issuance expected early next year. Unlike the 2025 plan, it doesn’t create a taxable district; Kansas City Public Schools says the deal is better but still short of its request, and early announcements will involve the planned Nordstrom site.
