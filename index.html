On This Day

November 16, 1907

Oklahoma becomes the 46th state. Oklahoma entered the Union as the forty-sixth state, merging Oklahoma Territory with what remained of Indian Territory. The land had been used from the 1830s to relocate the Choctaw, Chickasaw, Creek, Seminole and Cherokee nations, and Congress opened part of it to non-Native settlement in 1889. The state's name comes from the Choctaw words okla (people) and humma (red).

Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 16, 2026

travesty

noun · \TRAV-uh-stee\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Travesty refers to something that is shocking, upsetting, or ridiculous because it is not what it is supposed to be, but is instead a distorted or badly inferior imitation of it. The word is often used in the phrase “a travesty of.”

// The investigation into the causes of the accident was a complete travesty.

In context: “This case is an example of the failure of our judicial system’s lack of common sense, good judgment and morality. It is nothing less than a travesty of justice.” — Richard Votapka (letter to the editor), The Stuart (Florida) News, 18 Mar. 2026

Did you know? Disasters are tragedies, not travesties. A travesty specifically makes a mockery of what something is supposed to be: a contest won by the judge’s spouse, or a trial in which the defendant wasn’t allowed to present evidence. It comes from the French travestir, “to disguise,” and winds back through Italian to the Latin vestire, “to clothe”, which also gave English vestment, divest and invest.
