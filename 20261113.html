Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · November 13, 2026

impertinent

adjective · \im-PER-tuh-nunt\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Impertinent is a formal word describing someone or something that is rude and showing a lack of respect.

// The band stopped doing interviews after years of journalists asking invasive and impertinent questions.

In context: “… it would be impertinent to ask permission to fish on someone’s private property at such a late hour, so I decided to go by myself to the public section of the Soque River.” — Bill Hager, The White County (Georgia) News, 7 July 2026

Did you know? A pertinent remark is one that pertains to the subject at hand, so impertinent originally meant “irrelevant” (its oldest sense, from the 15th century). The rude sense is now more common: burping during a lecture is rude, but asking where your teacher bought such a ridiculous sweater is impertinent, because the question has nothing to do with the matter at hand.

New investment fund aims to raise $100 million for 5,000 affordable homes in KC region

The Kansas City office of LISC launched the Kansas City Regional Housing Fund, which will finance construction or preservation of up to 5,000 affordable units across nine Missouri and Kansas counties. Investors and foundations have already committed more than $25 million, and the region is short about 69,000 units for lower-income households.

At its first meeting since voting to close district schools, the Olathe board voted 5-0 (one member absent) on a required, non-binding step toward disposing of buildings including Black Bob Elementary and Indian Trail Middle School.
