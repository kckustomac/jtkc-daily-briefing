Word of the Day

Merriam-Webster · October 30, 2026

reticent

adjective · \RET-uh-sunt\

Audio: Merriam-Webster's official Word of the Day podcast episode (pronunciation and usage).

What it means: Someone may be described as reticent if they are feeling or showing reluctance, that is, hesitation or unwillingness to do something. Reticent can also describe someone who is reserved, inclined to be silent or uncommunicative about things.

// The team was reticent to spend such a large amount of money, even for an all-star free agent.

// He’s always been reticent about discussing personal matters at work.

In context: “It can be dangerous and difficult for journalists to report in war zones, and armies are understandably reticent to share details of their location, actions, and future plans.” — Rainey Reitman, Transaction Denied: Big Finance’s Power to Punish Speech, 2026

Did you know? Reticent comes from the Latin reticēre, “to keep silent,” and first meant “inclined to be silent or uncommunicative” in the early 19th century. The “reluctant” sense showed up only about 50 years later, first in contexts about speech (“reticent to talk about his past”), and is now well established. (Today’s horoscopes, which tell nearly everyone to open up, did not get the memo.)
