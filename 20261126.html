National Update

Holiday recess on Capitol Hill, a Dec. 11 funding cliff ahead

From C-SPAN

  • VP Vance Speaks to Reporters after Serving Thanksgiving Lunch to the Troops
  • NYC Mayor Mamdani Announces Postponement of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade
  • Tiana Lowe Doescher Discusses the Economy & Democrats' Agenda in the Next Congress (Washington Journal)

Congress: Congress is on Thanksgiving break with only brief pro forma sessions and no votes; the Senate is adjourned to noon Friday, and senators return for votes Monday, Nov. 30. The big deadline is Dec. 11, when federal funding expires: lawmakers must pass spending bills or a short-term extension to avoid a shutdown. The annual defense policy bill (NDAA) is also on the list but faces fights over Iran-war funding and the Pentagon budget. Democrats take control of both chambers in January, so a short stopgap into early 2027 looks more likely than a big deal.

Courts: In Brooklyn, prosecutors rested Nov. 25 in the federal fraud trial of Guardians pitchers Emmanuel Clase and Luis Ortiz over alleged rigged pitches and betting; it resumes Monday with the defense case and closings. In Buffalo, a jury was seated for the federal death-penalty trial of Payton Gendron, the 2022 Tops supermarket shooter, with openings set for Dec. 7. At the Supreme Court, the December session opens Monday and the AR-15 ban case (Khaya v. Cook County) is argued Dec. 2, with a ruling expected by late June.
